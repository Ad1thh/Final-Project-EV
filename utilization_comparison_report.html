1. Executive Summary & Research Scope

This utilization report presents a quantitative, dual-sided comparative evaluation between the reference publication "Selective hardening of RISCV soft-processors for space applications" by G. Cora, C. De Sio, S. Azimi, and L. Sterpone (Politecnico di Torino, 2025) and our RV32E Fault-Tolerant Soft-Core Architecture (Final-Project-EV).

The reference paper investigates the vulnerability profile of the NEORV32 soft-processor implemented on an AMD Xilinx Zynq-7020 SoC (XC7Z020) under radiation-induced Single Event Upsets (SEUs) and Multiple Bit Upsets (MBUs). Its central finding proves that applying monolithic Triple Modular Redundancy (TMR) across an entire processor results in an unacceptable 300% overhead penalty across logic, registers, and Block RAM. Instead, selective hardening applied to the most critical modules (the Control Unit and ALU) achieves near-identical fault mitigation while drastically reducing silicon resource consumption.

Our project adopts and extends this selective hardening paradigm, tailoring it to the rigorous demands of Electric Vehicle (EV) powertrain control and ISO 26262 ASIL-D functional safety. Rather than relying on blunt 3-way replication across the board, our architecture introduces a heterogeneous, energy-proportional fault-tolerance framework:

  • Adaptive Redundancy Controller (ARC) on ALU: Dynamically toggles between low-power Simplex execution and high-reliability TMR majority voting with operand isolation and glitch-free clock gating.
  • Dual Modular Redundancy (DMR) with Fail-Safe Clamping on Control Unit: Detects 100% of decoder anomalies via XOR shadow comparison and instantly clamps destructive write signals (safe_mem_write, safe_reg_write) to zero, avoiding voter gate delays on 10+ control wires while saving 33% area compared to CU TMR.
  • Triple Modular Redundancy (TMR) on Program Counter: 3-way replicated PC register bank with majority voting to guarantee continuous instruction sequencing.
  • Extended Hamming(38,32) SEC-DED ECC on Register File: Single-bit correction and double-bit error detection with only 21.8% bit overhead, avoiding the 200% storage overhead of register triplication.
  • Native Built-In ECC on Block RAM: Utilizing Xilinx RAMB36E1 dedicated hardware primitives, completely eliminating the 200% BRAM memory explosion reported in the paper.

4. Deep-Dive Comparative Factor Analysis

Factor 1: Control Unit (CU) Hardening — Fail-Safe Clamping vs. 3-Way Voting

Table 2 of the reference paper establishes that the Control Unit is the single most vulnerable entity in the processor, containing 238,327 Essential Bits (26.48% of the core total). In the paper, triplicating the CU increased LUT utilization from 6.02% to 14.23%. Furthermore, voting across 10+ control wires (reg_write, mem_write, alu_op[3:0], wb_sel[1:0], etc.) introduces severe critical-path gate delays and routing congestion.

Cora et al. Approach: Full CU TMR
  • Instantiates 3 complete Control Unit decoders.
  • Requires 3-way majority voters on every decoded wire.
  • Voter gate propagation delays worsen overall clock slack.
  • Consumes +8.21% additional LUTs across the FPGA fabric.
Our Approach: DMR with Fail-Safe Clamping
  • Instantiates 2 decoders (Primary + DMR Checker).
  • Single XOR tree detects any discrepancy between outputs.
  • Instantly clamps destructive signals (mem_write & ~cu_mismatch).
  • Saves 33% area compared to CU TMR; zero voter delay.

Factor 2: ALU Protection & Dynamic Energy Proportionality

Cora et al. demonstrated that ALU errors dominate floating-point workloads (reaching a 26.55% MBU error peak) but drop below 4% under integer workloads. However, their ALU triplication remains permanently active, continually dissipating dynamic switching power regardless of the workload phase.

Our architecture introduces the Adaptive Redundancy Controller (ARC) (adaptive_redundancy_controller.sv):

  • Hardware Pin / MMIO Mode Control: Real-time toggling between Simplex (Mode 0) and TMR (Mode 1).
  • Glitch-Free Integrated Clock Gating (ICG): Driven by active-low falling-edge latches (clock_gater.sv), cutting clocks to redundant execution units u_alu_1 and u_alu_2 during Simplex execution.
  • Operand Clamping: In Simplex mode, operands to redundant ALUs are clamped to 32'h0 and opcodes to neutral ALU_ADD, eliminating dynamic logic toggling and saving up to 66% ALU dynamic execution power.

Factor 3: Register File (RF) Protection — SEC-DED vs. Triplication

In Table 1 of Cora et al., the RF-BUS TMR configuration yielded negligible reliability benefits because triplicating 32 registers tripled storage flip-flops without addressing the vulnerability of the ALU or CU.

Our project deploys Extended Hamming(38,32) SEC-DED ECC on the 16-entry register file (regfile.sv):

  • Stores 39 bits per register (32 data bits + 6 Hamming parity bits + 1 overall parity bit).
  • Single Error Correction (SEC): Automatically calculates syndrome bits and flips corrupted bits on-the-fly with zero pipeline stalls.
  • Double Error Detection (DED): Detects uncorrectable double-bit flips and asserts telemetry flags (ecc_ded_1, ecc_ded_2).
  • Storage Efficiency: Incurs only 21.8% bit overhead (7 check bits / 32 data bits) compared to the 200% storage overhead of register triplication.

8. Conclusions & Strategic Engineering Takeaways

Conclusion 1: Validation of Selective Hardening
Both the reference publication and our experimental results confirm that monolithic TMR is an inefficient engineering choice. Triplicating an entire processor triples logic, registers, and memory without providing proportional reliability benefits over selective protection.
Conclusion 2: The Control Unit is the Core Linchpin
Cora et al. demonstrated that the Control Unit accounts for over 26% of all critical bits. Protecting only the datapath (ALU) while leaving the Control Unit vulnerable is dangerous. Our project's Dual Modular Redundancy (DMR) with fail-safe clamping achieves 100% write-corruption prevention while saving 33% silicon area compared to CU TMR and eliminating multi-signal voter propagation delays.
Conclusion 3: Memory Demands In-Tile ECC, Not Triplication
Cora et al.'s Full TMR design tripled Block RAM utilization from 9.64% to 28.93%. Our project demonstrates that utilizing dedicated hardware SEC-DED ECC (RAMB36E1) provides superior Single Event Upset correction with zero tile duplication overhead.
Conclusion 4: Dynamic Energy Proportionality (ARC)
Unlike the static redundancy of the reference paper, our Adaptive Redundancy Controller with glitch-free clock gating allows runtime switching between Simplex (low-power cruising) and TMR (safety-critical braking/steering) modes, cutting ALU dynamic power by 66% when full redundancy is unnecessary.
